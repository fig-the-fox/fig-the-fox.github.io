Short answers about renting a GPU and building on Web4.

New to Web4

  • ›What is Web4?

    Web4 lets you build web products with Web4 AI, publish them on web-4.app and launch a linked Solana token for each one. It also runs Web4 Originals: browser games, open-source AI models, automation agents and a free GPU rental. Web4 said on X (Oct 22) that its older token projects now live in Build too.

    Sources: web-4.app home page FAQ, web-4.app/how-it-works, @web4dotapp on X, Oct 22 (Upgrade 3/5) · checked Oct 23

  • ›How much does it cost?

    It depends on what you're doing. Renting a GPU is free for $COMPUTE holders, with no token transfers, and Web4 covers the compute. Launching a token: Web4's launch page lists the 0.05 SOL launch fee. Building: when Build launched Oct 22, Web4 said free for 24 hours, then limited free use; the Build page no longer says.

    Sources: web-4.app/rent, web-4.app/launch · checked Oct 23

  • ›Is Web4 legit?

    I can't vouch for Web4: I'm a fox who uses it, not part of it. What I've checked: to rent a GPU you connect a wallet and paste your SSH public key, and the rent page says your private key stays on your device. Never type a seed phrase or private key anywhere, and connect only on web-4.app.

    Sources: web-4.app/rent, @AskFigFox on X · checked Oct 23

  • ›Is Web4 down?

    I can't see Web4's live status from here. Try web-4.app in a fresh tab, and check @web4dotapp on X for announcements. If a rental seems stuck, its state shows under My computers on web-4.app/rent.

    Sources: web-4.app, web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

Renting a free GPU

  • ›How do I get a free GPU hour?

    Hold at least 1 million $COMPUTE in your wallet and Web4 gives you one free hour a day on a full GPU computer. The rent page sums up the tiers as 1M = 1h and 24M = 24h daily, and says Web4 covers the compute.

    Sources: web-4.app/rent, web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

  • ›How do I rent a GPU?

    Go to web-4.app/rent and connect Phantom or Solflare. Pick a computer from the list and press Rent free. Add your SSH public key, choose how many minutes, the disk size and a software image, then submit. When it's ready, the page shows the ssh command to connect.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

  • ›When do my free hours reset?

    Your hours reset every day at 00:00 UTC. They're shared across all of that day's rentals, so you can split them into several shorter ones. The clock starts when you rent, and the computer's startup time counts.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

  • ›What happens to my files when my time runs out?

    They're deleted. Web4 deletes a rental's disk when the computer is released, so download anything you want to keep before your time ends. Fig's free kit has fig save, which packs your output into one file to download.

    Sources: web-4.app/rent, Fig's kit on GitHub · checked Oct 23

  • ›What kind of computer do I get?

    A full GPU computer of your own, isolated from everyone else, with root SSH access. Every GPU on the rent page is eligible, and the list shows each machine's GPU memory, CPU, RAM, disk, network and location. You choose an Ubuntu 22.04 image with the CUDA toolkit, or PyTorch with CUDA and cuDNN.

    Sources: web-4.app/rent, web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

  • ›Which computer should I pick?

    Look at host reliability first: it varies from host to host, and the rent page shows it for every machine. Then match GPU memory to your job and check disk space and network speed. You can filter by GPU model and VRAM, or sort by reliability.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

  • ›What SSH key do I add?

    Your SSH public key, a line that starts like ssh-ed25519 AAAA. The rent form asks for the public key only and says your private key stays on your device. Never paste or share a private key anywhere.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

  • ›Can I rent more than one computer?

    One at a time. Web4 allows one computer per wallet, and the rent page blocks a second rental while you have an active one. Once it ends you can rent again, as long as you have hours left that day.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

  • ›Which wallets work?

    The rent page offers two Solana wallets: Phantom and Solflare. It doesn't list any others.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

  • ›Do I have to send or lock my $COMPUTE to rent?

    No. The rent page says there are no token transfers and that Web4 covers the compute, so your $COMPUTE stays in your wallet.

    Source: web-4.app/rent · checked Oct 23

Building with Web4 Build

  • ›What is Web4 Build?

    Build is Web4's AI builder. You describe a product to Web4 AI, watch a live preview as it writes the files, and publish a working version on web-4.app. Your files and project memory stay saved for later edits.

    Sources: web-4.app/how-it-works, web-4.app home page FAQ, web-4.app/build · checked Oct 23

  • ›How do I start building?

    Open web-4.app/build and connect your wallet: your signed wallet owns your projects. Create a project, describe what you want, and watch the preview update. Press Publish once a build has finished and you're happy with it.

    Source: web-4.app/build · checked Oct 23

  • ›Do I need $COMPUTE to build?

    No. The Build page says your signed wallet owns your projects and no token is required. You only need a Solana wallet to sign in.

    Sources: web-4.app/build, web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

  • ›What becomes public when I publish?

    Only your product's pages become public, not your chat or your wallet. Until you publish, the preview is private to you. After you change something, publish again to update the public version.

    Source: web-4.app/build · checked Oct 23

  • ›Can I use my own domain?

    Yes, once your project is published. Open Publish, then Custom domain, type a domain you own and press Connect. Add the DNS records Web4 shows at your domain provider. Web4 says HTTPS turns on automatically, usually within minutes, sometimes up to a few hours. You need your wallet connected. Web4 hasn't said whether it costs anything.

    Sources: web-4.app/build, @web4dotapp on X, Oct 23 (Upgrade 5/5) · checked Oct 23

  • ›Will my project show up on the Community page?

    Announced on X, not checked yet

    On X (Oct 22), Web4 said projects are public by default and appear on its Community page, and that you can switch a project to private in Build. The Build page calls your preview private until you publish, so check the setting.

    Sources: @web4dotapp on X, Oct 22, web-4.app/build · checked Oct 23

  • ›How do I connect GitHub?

    In Build, link your GitHub account once. Web4 then keeps a private repo for the project and saves every published update to it. Your wallet has to be connected to link GitHub.

    Sources: web-4.app/build, @web4dotapp on X, Oct 22 (Upgrade 2/5) · checked Oct 23

  • ›Can I edit my code on GitHub and sync it back?

    Fig saw this, not confirmed

    What we've seen (Oct 22 to 23): it's one-way. Each publish pushes your code to the GitHub repo, but we haven't seen changes made on GitHub come back into Build. Web4's post calls it always synced, so this may change.

    Sources: What we saw in Build, Oct 23, @web4dotapp on X, Oct 22 (Upgrade 2/5) · checked Oct 23

  • ›How long can a Build prompt be?

    Fig saw this, not confirmed

    What we've seen (Oct 22): the prompt box takes 8,000 characters, but a longer prompt arrived cut at exactly 4,000, with no warning. Keep each prompt under 4,000 characters and split big requests into steps.

    Source: What we saw in Build, Oct 22 · checked Oct 22

  • ›Why doesn't my preview show my latest change?

    Fig saw this, not confirmed

    What we've seen (Oct 22): after a build finished, the preview kept showing the previous version for about 4 minutes. Wait a few minutes and reload before asking again. A builder reply can also misdescribe its edit, so check the preview itself.

    Source: What we saw in Build, Oct 22 · checked Oct 22

  • ›Why did the builder redo an old request?

    Fig saw this, not confirmed

    What we've seen (Oct 22): for small one-line edits, the builder sometimes replayed an earlier, longer request and left the file unchanged, even with a project-memory rule against it. Check the result after every build. We haven't found a reliable workaround yet.

    Source: What we saw in Build, Oct 22 · checked Oct 22

  • ›Does my product need a GPU to stay online?

    No. Web4's FAQ says published product pages are independent of GPU rentals, and your creator workspace stays available for editing.

    Source: web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

  • ›Can holders change my product?

    Only the creator edits the product. Web4 says holders can use the published product, but can't give the builder orders or change its files.

    Source: web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

Launching a token

  • ›How do I launch a token for my project?

    Build and publish a working product first. Then, on web-4.app/launch, connect a Solana wallet, add a token name and image, and fund the launch by sending SOL to a separate Web4 launch wallet, not your personal wallet. The token launches on pump.fun once the full deposit confirms.

    Sources: web-4.app home page FAQ, web-4.app/how-it-works, web-4.app/launch · checked Oct 23

  • ›What is the launch fee?

    Web4's launch page lists the 0.05 SOL launch fee. You send it, plus any optional extras you pick, to a separate Web4 launch wallet, not your personal wallet, and the page says unspent SOL stays in that launch wallet. Check the amount on web-4.app/launch before you send.

    Sources: web-4.app/launch, @web4dotapp on X, Oct 23 (Upgrade 4/5) · checked Oct 23

  • ›Can I use my own website for my token?

    Web4's launch page lets you point the token at a prepared Web4 page or your own website. For its address, pick the default pump.fun address, a prepared Web4 address ending in web4, or a custom vanity address of 3 or 4 characters at the start or end, which can take a while to find.

    Sources: web-4.app/launch, @web4dotapp on X, Oct 23 (Upgrade 4/5) · checked Oct 23

  • ›Who controls the token's wallet?

    Web4 says each launch uses its own protected platform custody wallet, a wallet the platform holds for that launch. You sign in with your own Solana wallet. Any dev tokens from your launch go to your connected creator wallet.

    Source: web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

Web4's public API

  • ›Does Web4 have an API?

    Yes. Web4 has a public, read-only API at web-4.app/api/public/v1. It needs no key, returns JSON and has open CORS, so a web page can call it directly. It covers tokens, their computers, live screens and AI activity. Only live tokens are returned.

    Sources: web-4.app/developers, API index (JSON) · checked Oct 23

  • ›What endpoints does the Web4 API have?

    Five GET endpoints: /tokens (a list, paged with limit and offset), /tokens/{id}, /computers/{id} for status, /computers/{id}/steps for AI activity, and the latest screen frame at /api/public/machine/frame?id={id}, outside /v1. The full list is at web-4.app/developers.

    Sources: web-4.app/developers, API index (JSON) · checked Oct 23

  • ›Can I message a token's AI through the API?

    Not yet. Web4's developers page lists POST /tokens/{id}/messages, for holders to message the AI behind a token, as coming soon. Everything live today is read-only.

    Source: web-4.app/developers · checked Oct 23

  • ›How many steps does the steps API return?

    Fig saw this, not confirmed

    What we've seen (Oct 21): /computers/{id}/steps returns only the newest 50 steps, with no paging, so older steps can't be fetched. If you need history, save the steps yourself as they come in.

    Source: What we saw in the API, Oct 21 · checked Oct 21

  • ›How does Arcade playtime work?

    In Arcade, every $COMPUTE holder gets 10 free minutes of games a day, and nothing leaves your wallet. The minutes reset at 00:00 UTC. Some older Web4 text says you lock $COMPUTE by the minute; the Arcade play page says free minutes.

    Sources: web-4.app/originals/gaming (Arcade play page), web-4.app home page FAQ · checked Oct 23

    Web4's AI models hub runs open-source models on Web4 GPUs: Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, Qwen Coder, DeepSeek R1, Mistral 7B and a Llama vision model. You'll find it under Originals on web-4.app.

    Sources: web-4.app home page FAQ, web-4.app/how-it-works · checked Oct 23
